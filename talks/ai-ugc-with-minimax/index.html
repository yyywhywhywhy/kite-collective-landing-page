1Find a winning video

Start from a proven winner.

I found this "Amazon finds" glassware video on TikTok. A simple four-beat structure, and not one word spoken.

  • Same product type as yours (glassware → wine glasses)
  • A clear structure you can see in the stills
  • The product in use is the star
2Break it down with Claude

Second by second, what happens.

Download the video, give it to Claude, and ask for a timestamped breakdown: every cut, every shot, the camera angle, the light.

Then ask it to rewrite each beat for your product.

0–6sThe closed boxHeld still for two seconds. Hands lift the lid. This is the hook.
6–10sThe revealGlasses in a row on the table. A hand turns one into the light.
10–14sThe heroOne glass lifted to the window. Light shines through the colours.
14–19sThe payoffPull back to the person. She raises the glass, sips, smiles.
3Create the character & scene

Generate the person. Bring in your product.

Use GPT (or any image model) to make a still of your customer and your product in their home. This locks the look before you spend on video.

  • Start from your real product photo
  • Describe the customer in detail: age, hair, clothes, jewellery
  • Pick the still where the product looks most like itself

One prompt per beat. Then stitch.

Feed Claude's description of each beat, plus your images, into MiniMax H3. Here each beat is its own clip, joined in Higgsfield. H3 can also hold several shots in one 15-second generation.

Ethics & best practice

AI is synthetic. Don't fake the result.

✕ Never

Fake efficacy

  • Generated "after" results for skincare or supplements
  • A made-up customer claiming it changed their life
  • One backlash can destroy the trust your brand built
✓ Fair game

Show what's real

  • How it looks, styled in a home (glasses, décor, fashion)
  • What the product really does
  • Follow each platform's rules for labelling AI-generated content
